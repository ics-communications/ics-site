# Claude Code Brief: New Draft of the ICS MA and PhD Website

## Assignment

Create a new, polished draft of the Institute for Christian Studies website for its MA and PhD programs. Begin by inspecting the existing project and its conventions. Then redesign and rewrite the relevant pages using this brief as the governing content and strategy document.

The earlier interactive draft can be viewed here:

<https://usepastel.com/link/37j71xd3/#/ma-phd-landing.html>

Do not simply reproduce that draft. Retain its strongest ideas, visual language, and useful content structure, but address the strategic and factual concerns below. The new version should feel like a substantive second draft.

## Central Strategic Direction

The site must not present ICS only as a philosophy school. The official degrees may continue to be called the MA in Philosophy and PhD in Philosophy, but the wider educational identity should be framed as **spiritually attuned, culturally relevant Christian scholarship**.

This distinction is essential because a significant portion of the full-time faculty teaches theology and biblical studies rather than philosophy. An exclusively philosophical identity would misrepresent the faculty and unnecessarily exclude potential students.

The website should communicate that ICS is a place where rigorous scholarship, Christian faith, and urgent cultural questions meet. It should show that students are formed through close intellectual apprenticeship to pursue wisdom for the whole of life.

An appropriate high-level formulation is:

> Spiritually attuned, culturally relevant Christian scholarship addressing the deepest questions of human life.

Use this as strategic guidance, not necessarily as mandatory final copy. Develop strong, concise language suited to prospective graduate students.

## What Makes ICS Distinctive

The website should make the following case clearly and concretely:

1. **Scholarship connected to life:** ICS resists scholarship that retreats into narrow specialization. Students connect rigorous theoretical reflection to personal, ecclesial, institutional, and cultural problems.
2. **Faith as intellectually generative:** Christian faith is not added to otherwise neutral scholarship. It shapes the questions, practices, and purposes of scholarship from the beginning.
3. **Spiritual depth and cultural relevance:** ICS seeks scholarship that is spiritually serious while remaining socially practicable and publicly meaningful.
4. **Intellectual apprenticeship:** Small seminars, close supervision, and relationships between Junior Members and Senior Members distinguish ICS from larger graduate institutions.
5. **Interdisciplinarity:** The programs draw upon philosophy, theology, biblical studies, aesthetics, politics, education, hermeneutics, and related fields.
6. **Online accessibility:** The programs are primarily online and can be pursued by students outside Toronto. Toronto, the Toronto School of Theology, and the Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam remain important academic connections, but the copy must not imply that most students live and study in Toronto.
7. **Small institution, substantial influence:** ICS has historically exercised influence beyond its size through its graduates, scholarship, organizations, and role in Christian intellectual life.

## Primary Audiences

Write for prospective students who may include:

- students preparing for academic research or teaching;
- teachers, ministers, writers, institutional leaders, and public intellectuals;
- people seeking advanced Christian scholarship for personal or vocational formation;
- students interested in theology, biblical studies, culture, politics, education, aesthetics, or religion, not only students who already identify as philosophers;
- international and geographically dispersed students looking for a rigorous online graduate program;
- prospective MA students who may not yet have a direct connection to ICS or Reformational philosophy.

Do not assume that all PhD applicants intend to become professors. Academic vocation remains important, but the language should not exclude students pursuing advanced study for intellectual, spiritual, personal, or other professional reasons.

## Tone and Copy Style

The copy should be:

- intellectually serious without being opaque;
- distinctively Christian without relying on generic religious slogans;
- confident but factually restrained;
- warm, invitational, and personal;
- oriented toward questions, formation, and scholarly practice, not merely career outcomes;
- specific enough that ICS does not sound like a generic graduate school.

Avoid inflated or unverifiable claims. In particular:

- Do not claim that ICS offers “generous financial aid.” Emphasize affordable tuition and describe only verified forms of assistance.
- Do not imply that students must relocate to Toronto.
- Do not claim a winter admissions deadline unless current policy is confirmed.
- Do not invent statistics, student quotations, faculty credentials, course offerings, scholarships, deadlines, or admissions requirements.

Explain the terms **Junior Member** and **Senior Member** once. Use them naturally afterward, but do not let internal terminology obscure the meaning for new visitors.

## Messaging to Preserve or Develop

The earlier draft contained several strong ideas worth retaining and refining:

- “Find Your Scholarly Voice” was especially well received as the MA heading.
- The MA was effectively described as guiding students into integrally Christian scholarship for the academy, classroom, or public square.
- “Scholarship as a Practice of Wisdom” is a valuable concept, although it should be brought into conversation with “spiritually attuned, culturally relevant scholarship.”
- “Learning is apprenticeship, not transaction” captures something important about the ICS model.
- The strongest version of the site will combine intellectual apprenticeship, spiritual depth, cultural engagement, and rigorous scholarship.

Consider a hero formulation that removes unnecessary repetition of “philosophy.” For example:

> Graduate programs for scholars and seekers called to pursue wisdom at the intersection of faith and the deepest questions of human life.

This example may be edited, but its breadth should be preserved.

If the current project includes additional pages such as an MA FAQ, program-design page, application page, or field-of-study pages, bring them into the same messaging system and correct any contradictions.

## Required Factual Corrections

Treat the following as corrections requested by the MA and PhD Program Director. Where a statement is marked for verification, do not guess. Use an explicit placeholder or omit the contested detail until authoritative information is available.

### MA program

- The MA can be completed part-time.
- All ICS courses are seminars. Do not create a false distinction between “courses” and “seminars.”
- Describe the curriculum as **three foundation courses, six electives, and one capstone interdisciplinary seminar**.
- Use the same curriculum description everywhere, including cards, tables, timelines, and FAQs.
- Feature representative courses taught regularly by current full-time Senior Members. Do not build the principal course showcase around emeritus or sessional instructors.
- Appropriate examples to consider include Neal DeRoo’s Phenomenology or *Material Spirituality* courses and an elective taught by Ron Kuipers. Verify exact titles and current scheduling before presenting them as forthcoming courses.
- Describe the intended audience broadly, including people interested in spiritually attuned, culturally relevant scholarship and people seeking to explore the spiritual roots of contemporary cultural issues.

### PhD program

- Part-time PhD study is available, including during the coursework phase.
- If the program is described as two years of coursework plus three years of dissertation work, specify that this is a **full-time** five-year pathway.
- Do not imply that all PhD students intend to become professors.
- Review any claim that applicants must hold an MA with a thesis component. The Program Director questioned whether that is actually required and warned that it could unnecessarily exclude qualified applicants. Mark this as **VERIFY WITH ACADEMIC OFFICE**.
- Explain the candidacy or comprehensive examination as testing a student’s readiness to begin dissertation research and their mastery of the broad areas engaged by the proposed dissertation.
- Foreign-language competency is a requirement for graduation or program completion, not a prerequisite for admission. Students do not necessarily need the relevant languages before entering the program.

### Online, international, and conjoint study

- Make clear that the MA and PhD programs can be pursued online.
- At the time of the Program Director’s review, ICS could not offer student visas, meaning international students needed to study online. Mark the exact current wording as **VERIFY BEFORE PUBLICATION**.
- The VU conjoint agreement technically includes a six-month residency requirement. State the formal requirement accurately. Do not promise that it will be waived, even if waivers may sometimes be possible. Mark the final wording as **VERIFY AGAINST THE CURRENT ICS–VU AGREEMENT**.

### Tuition, financial aid, and deadlines

- Replace “generous financial aid” with accurate language about affordable tuition and any specific verified assistance.
- Do not invent scholarship amounts or availability.
- Do not publish a November 1 winter admissions deadline without confirmation. Historically, financial-aid decisions were organized around a once-per-year process, even though students might begin individual courses at other times.
- Mark all dates and financial-aid procedures as **VERIFY WITH ACADEMIC OFFICE**.

### Fields of study

- Ensure that the stated number of fields matches the number actually displayed.
- Do not use “Religious Studies” casually as a field title. It denotes an established discipline, including comparative and cross-cultural study of religion, that ICS may not be equipped to claim in full.
- Review the official fields and choose an accurate title related to religion, philosophy of religion, spirituality, or theological reflection only after consulting the authoritative program language.
- Preserve the idea of overlapping, interdisciplinary “neighbourhoods” if it remains useful, but do not let creative naming obscure the actual curriculum.

### Faculty information

- Neal DeRoo teaches a course titled *Material Spirituality: Re-Thinking Religion*. Remove any course falsely attributed to him.
- A second course in the earlier draft was also incorrectly attributed to Neal. Verify all course lists rather than trying to infer which title is correct.
- Add **political phenomenology** to Neal’s research areas.
- Add Neal’s book *Material Spirituality: A Transcendental Phenomenology of Religion* (Bloomsbury, 2026).
- Rebekah Smick should appear in the emeritus section rather than among active full-time faculty, subject to confirmation of her current title.
- Review every faculty image. One image in the earlier draft prompted a joking response from Neal and was likely an obvious placeholder or mismatch.

Potential themes include:

- ICS’s role in developing and disseminating the Christian worldview tradition;
- the conviction that following Christ transforms the whole of life rather than only explicitly ecclesial activity;
- the influence of ICS graduates and associated scholars in churches, schools, public institutions, and Christian organizations;

## Maintainability Requirement

The Program Director expects the positioning of the programs to evolve as the faculty changes. The site must therefore be easy to update without redesigning every page.

Where compatible with the existing stack:

- separate program content from layout and presentation;
- centralize shared facts such as degree names, curriculum structure, delivery modes, deadlines, tuition language, and calls to action;
- centralize faculty profiles, research areas, publications, and course lists;
- create reusable components for program cards, course cards, faculty profiles, FAQ items, testimonials, and outcome statistics;
- avoid repeating policy language manually across several files;
- make sections with unavailable data easy to hide rather than filling them with invented content.

The site should be capable of shifting toward a more philosophy-centred identity in the future if faculty composition changes, without requiring a complete structural rebuild.

## Design and User Experience

The visual direction should convey intellectual seriousness, warmth, and depth. Avoid a generic corporate-university appearance.

Requirements:

- responsive layouts across desktop, tablet, and mobile;
- semantic HTML and accessible heading structure;
- keyboard-accessible navigation and controls;
- sufficient colour contrast;
- useful alternative text for meaningful images;
- restrained motion that respects reduced-motion settings;
- clear calls to action without placing an application button after every section;
- scannable program comparisons without oversimplifying important differences;
- fast loading and minimal unnecessary dependencies;
- no broken internal links or placeholder links presented as complete.

Use authentic ICS photography and approved faculty images when they exist in the project. If suitable images are unavailable, use clearly identified development placeholders rather than treating stock photography as final.

## Implementation Process

1. Inspect the repository, framework, existing routes, components, assets, and content sources.
2. Identify duplicated or contradictory program facts.
3. Produce a short implementation plan before making major structural changes.
4. Build the revised pages and reusable content system.
5. Preserve unrelated existing functionality and user-authored changes.
6. Run the project’s available formatting, linting, type-checking, and build commands.
7. Test the main user journeys and all internal navigation.
8. Report any claims that remain unverified and identify exactly where placeholders remain.

Do not stop after producing recommendations. Implement the new draft in the repository unless a genuine technical or factual blocker prevents it.

## Acceptance Criteria

The new draft is successful when:

- a visitor understands within the first screen that ICS offers rigorous Christian graduate scholarship without being told that ICS is only for philosophers;
- the MA and PhD have distinct purposes but share a coherent institutional identity;
- online and part-time options are visible and accurately described;
- no page makes unsupported claims about financial aid, deadlines, visas, residency, placement, or admissions requirements;
- the MA curriculum is consistent everywhere;
- the PhD timeline clearly identifies its full-time assumption;
- the site welcomes future academics without excluding other serious doctoral students;
- the faculty information is accurate and maintainable;
- testimonials and placement evidence can be added without fabricating temporary content;
- the design feels distinctive to ICS rather than like a generic university template;
- content likely to change is centralized and can be updated safely.
