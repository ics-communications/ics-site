Program Design

A detailed look at the structure, courses, and timeline of the two-year MA in Philosophy.

Apply Today → About the MA

Program Structure

Ten Courses, One Thesis, One Voice

The MA program guides you from broad foundations to focused expertise. A Junior Member begins with three Foundations Courses and concludes with an oral defense of their written thesis. In between, you develop a reading list, demonstrate language competence, and write a major scholarly work.

Foundations

The Shared Starting Point

Three courses completed near the beginning of your program provide an overarching, biblically-directed philosophical and spiritual frame for the scholarly community at ICS.

Biblical Foundations

The biblical story from Genesis to Revelation as the grounding narrative for ICS’s scholarly questions and concerns.

Philosophical Foundations

Critiques of foundationalism, metaphysics, and modernity within Reformational philosophy and other schools of thought.

Spiritual Foundations

A spiritual frame for the practice of Christian scholarship within a community of faith and learning.

Elective Courses

Six Courses in Your Field of Study

Mentor-Guided

Chosen in consultation with your Senior Member Mentor, normally three per academic year.

ICS & TST

Draw from ICS course offerings or up to two courses from the Toronto School of Theology.

Credit Transfers

Transfer up to two graduate-level courses from other institutions with approval.

Seven Fields

Courses fall within seven interdisciplinary fields of study, from aesthetics to theology to political philosophy.

Capstone

The MA Thesis

Junior Members work closely with their Mentor to develop a reading list in their intended thesis area, draft a proposal, and write and formally defend their thesis. The thesis demonstrates competence in the chosen area.

  • Length: 17,000–25,000 words
  • Guided Reading: Developing expertise in your thesis area
  • Proposal: Formal thesis proposal approved by your committee
  • Oral Examination: Conducted by the thesis committee, exploring your knowledge
